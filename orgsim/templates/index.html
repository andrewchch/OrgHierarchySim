Experiment 01  ·  hierarchy formation

Do career ladders keep bad hierarchies alive?

A population of workers with different skills, ambitions and loyalty competes for jobs. Firms hire, promote, grow and fail. The question is whether a taste for promotion can make manager-heavy organisations survive even when they produce less per worker.

Open the simulator

Working hypothesis

When workers value promotion prospects, tall firms can pay less for the same talent. That career-ladder subsidy lets them carry more managers — and more bureaucracy — than a purely productivity-driven market would tolerate.

career preference → lower wages → taller firms survive

The simulator is built so this can fail: competition, overhead and promotion ceilings all push in the opposite direction.

Model notes

What is being simulated

agent-based

Workers

Each agent has skill, ambition, hierarchy support, mobility and a behavioural archetype. They compare wage, promotion prospects, hierarchy affinity and switching costs.

Firms

Firms inherit a span-of-control strategy. Growth creates managerial slots; retention promotions can create extra layers. Payroll, overhead and coordination losses determine profit.

Selection

Output markets set a price. Weak firms shrink and fail, strong firms hire and spin out entrants. Strategies are inherited with mutation, so the population of organisations evolves.

Identification

The counterfactual changes only the value workers place on promotion prospects and retention promotions. Everything else is held constant for the same seed structure.
